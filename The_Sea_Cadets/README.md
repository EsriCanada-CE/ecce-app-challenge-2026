# Carbon Emission for Transport Characteristics

This ECCE app helps people make smarter and more sustainable travel
choices. Users can enter where they want to start and finish, or use the
find my location feature, and quickly get a route with an estimate of
carbon emissions. This way, the app makes it easy to see and understand
each trip’s environmental impact.

The app also includes a ‘Near Me’ feature for finding the closest
amenities, such as bike racks, repair shops, washrooms, accessible
parking, and streetlights, making it easier to meet real travel needs
such as rest, safety, and accessibility.

By bringing together route planning, environmental information, and
nearby amenities in one place, the app helps users find efficient,
comfortable, and accessible routes that suit their needs.

Overall, our app stands out because it turns complex travel and
environmental data into simple, useful information. This helps people
choose travel options that are healthier, safer, and better for the
environment.

# Hub Page Description

## Why Transit in the HRM

Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere, leading to ocean
acidification, extreme weather, and global warming. In a growing battle
to curb global warming, transportation is responsible for around 4.6
metric tonnes of carbon dioxide (CO2) per person each year. That
accounts for over 15% of global greenhouse gas emissions!

Less than a month ago (March 2026), the Government of Nova Scotia had an
annual budget reevaluation, which resulted in the cut of the ‘Halifax
Free Student Transit Pass’ program. This \$600,000 cut affected over
30,000 Halifax students in grades 12 to 17, raising concerns over
independent mobility and sustainable transportation around the city.
These cuts reduce the accessibility of school, employment, and social
opportunities for Halifax youth. Furthermore, in light of the oncoming
climate crisis, reducing the affordability of public transit could lead
to 1) more reliance on personal vehicles 2) greater traffic congestion
3) an increase in CO2 emissions.

A recent 25.3% budget cut to Nova Scotia’s Department of Environment and
Climate Change adds even more concern and uncertainty to the way that
the province is choosing to handle environmental changes.

As such, we have chosen to build a software to suggest alternate modes
of sustainable transportation and raise community awareness regarding
individual carbon footprints. We hope to promote sustainable forms of
transportation (EVs, public transportation, biking, and walking) where
possible and suggest related amenities that reduce potential barriers to
the use of aforementioned methods.

## Featured Amenities of Our Tracker App

### Bike racks

were included to allow app users to plan around sustainable
transportation use and decrease the time and uncertainty barrier with
regards to securing their bike near the desired destination.

### Bike repair shops

were included especially for those who may plan to travel longer
distances. Knowing where the nearest repair location is—should anything
happen—could increase bike usership and security during longer commutes.

### Accessible parking spots

are present to accommodate any accessibility needs for car and EV users.
Those who require these services will be able to see the location of
accessible parking spots around their destination and plan accordingly.

### Park and pay stations

include all paid parking areas (not zones) in the HRM, and they were
included to address the time barrier associated with parking, especially
in densely built areas like downtown Halifax (where a lot of HRM
residents need to commute to).

### EV charging stations

provide EV users with reassurance that they will be able to supply their
vehicles with energy whatever their destination in the HRM. Having
direct access to this information while route planning can increase
safety and longevity of EV trips, which could promote the use of these
vehicles.

### Park and Ride

promotes public transportation use for users who live further away from
downtown Halifax. These Park and Ride areas allow users from less
densely populated areas that do not have a high volume of public
transport servicing to station their vehicle while they take the bus
towards inner Halifax. Park and Ride promotes public transportation use
for those who do not live in central Halifax and helps lower overall
emissions and traffic.

### Sidewalk street lights

are an essential amenity for ensuring the safety and comfort of
pedestrians. Having well–lit walking areas could not only support
Halifax pedestrians but also decrease reliance on vehicle use (i.e. Uber
use) for night journeys.

### Washrooms

were included to support commuters of all kinds and to provide families
(especially with smaller children) a constant contingency plan on their
trip.

### A note on non-electric vehicles

Although they are not classified as sustainable, we included gasoline
and diesel cars as a potential transport option in our app because we
did not want to compromise transport accessibility. Electric vehicles
(EVs) are costly means of transportation, and users from different
socioeconomic backgrounds may not have a way of accessing this
sustainable transport with its current costs.

## How We Calculated Emissions

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) suggests that each
person emits no more than 2,000 kg of CO2 per year to limit the global
temperature increase to 1.5°C. This number is equivalent to
approximately 5.5 kg of CO2 per person every day.

Bus: total CO2 emissions (kg) = distance (km) \* 1.2 Total CO2 emissions
per person: total CO2 emissions/15

Other transit: total CO2 emissions= (distance/1000) \* conversion factor
