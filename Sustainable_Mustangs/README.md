## Mission Statement

Since being coined in 2016 by Professor Carlos Morenos, the concept of 15-minute cities has taken urban planning by storm[1]. It describes a phenomenon where neighbourhoods are planned to allow residents to reach all their daily needs within a 15-minute walk. The aim of our app INSERT APP NAME is to highlight which areas in London that have the greatest access to everyday amenities. Additionally, as city buses are a vital form of transportation within London, our team has expanded this idea to also analyse which bus stops are closest to important destinations. We have included data on access to parks, healthcare centres, grocery stores, convenience stores, and schools. This will help residents to recognize desirable neighbourhoods within their city, while also allowing policy makers to identify gaps within the city’s infrastructure.  

Source: Meyers, S. (2023). "Myths and benefits of the 15-minute city." Retrieved March 1, 2026, from https://www.ucalgary.ca/news/myths-and-benefits-15-minute-city.

## Statement of Characteristics

Our app, "15-Minute London" is built to be information and user-friendly. It contains two different maps: One showing how walkable different neighbourhoods are, and another that displays which bus routes have greatest access to important amenities. This recognizes the importance of both walking and buses as forms of sustainable transportation within the day-to-day lives of Londoners. With our app, users will be able to assess the walkeability of different neighbourhoods and the efficiency of each bus line, allowing them to make informed decisions on where to live, which route to take, and what to advocate for within their city. Furthermore, decision-makers, like politicians, urban planners, and other city employees, can consult this app to evaluate which areas are best-served by amenities, and which ones require more development. They can also identify weaker routes within the London Transit Commission.

## Exploring the App

When you launch into the app, you will first be greeted by a pop-up. Then, you will see two maps, a description, and some relevant statistics. 
In the first map, showing walkability, users can search for their address to see directly if their home is in a walkable location. In the second map, they can discover how many amenities each individual London bus route can take them to. There is a legend to the side of this map that highlights the accessibility to necessary infrastructure of each bus route in an intuitive, colour-coded list. 
Additionally, users can customize their experience by toggling with the size of the maps. By clicking and dragging the edge of a map, its size can be changed to make it larger, smaller, or have it completely hidden. This allows visitors to focus on their map of interest.
